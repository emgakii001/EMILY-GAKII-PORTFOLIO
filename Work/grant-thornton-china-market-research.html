Overview

My role in the project

Grant Thornton China was exploring how to expand its Forensic Advisory Services practice. As Outreach Consultant, I supported this across the full business-development arc — from open-ended market research, through screening and prioritization, to designing an engagement plan for two of the firm's key clients.

My work spanned three connected stages: identifying candidate organizations through secondary research, evaluating and ranking them against defined criteria, and mapping specific client needs to Grant Thornton's service lines with a plan to deepen those relationships over time.

“Grant Thornton China's forensic advisory practice lacked a systematic way to identify who had a real need for its services, rank them by value, and turn that ranking into concrete engagement action.”
— The business problem this project addressed
Research & Approach

From an open market to a resourced pipeline

The work moved through a defined sequence — each stage narrowing the previous one into something more actionable.

Market research

I researched candidate organizations across five categories of public sources — legal directories, government regulatory databases, law firm websites, industry rankings (Forbes Global 2000), and financial/private-markets databases (Preqin, PitchBook, S&P Global). For each, I logged entity type, global presence, confirmed China office location, and a rationale for relevance to forensic advisory services.

Legal directories Government databases Law firm websites Industry rankings PE/VC databases

Opportunity screening

Not every internationally-present company is a good prospect. I applied six qualifying criteria to determine shortlist eligibility — all 30 shortlisted firms were validated against all six.

Global presence Beijing/Shanghai office Regulatory exposure (FCPA / UK Bribery Act / PRC ACL) Approved client category Identifiable FAS service need Reachable decision maker

Client & stakeholder prioritization

I plotted all 30 shortlisted organizations on a Power/Interest Grid to decide who to pursue first — sorting a flat list into a resourced sequence with a distinct engagement plan for each group. See the full grid below.

Use case mapping

For two flagship clients already in a relationship with Grant Thornton — Microsoft Corporation and The Carlyle Group — I mapped their specific risk profile to Grant Thornton's forensic service lines through a step-by-step use case, connecting who the client is, how the engagement runs, and what service is delivered.

Retention strategy design

For Microsoft and Carlyle, I designed five tailored retention strategies each — covering onboarding, ongoing communication, feedback loops, and subscription-style service models — intended to move each relationship from a single engagement toward a recurring one.

Key Findings

What the research showed

＋

Regulatory exposure is a reliable screening signal. Using FCPA, UK Bribery Act, and PRC Anti-Corruption Law exposure to qualify prospects separated companies with a plausible forensic-services need from those that merely had a China presence.

＋

Prioritization turns research into resourcing. Sorting the shortlist by power and interest converted a flat list of 30 names into a sequence — who gets a two-week tailored proposal, and who gets a quarterly check-in.

＋

Use-case mapping is where the value shows up. A shortlist tells you who to approach; connecting a prioritized name to an actual step-by-step service use case tells you what to do next.

＋

Prospecting and retention are distinct exercises. The use-case and retention work focused on deepening two existing client relationships — a different objective from the broader shortlist-building stage, though both feed the same overall pipeline.
