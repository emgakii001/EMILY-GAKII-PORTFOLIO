01 — The Challenge

Knowing how many learners signed up wasn't the same as knowing what was actually happening.

Excelerate runs learning opportunities — internships, courses, events, competitions, and engagements — for students and early-career professionals worldwide. Years of participation data had piled up, but nobody had turned it into a clear picture of where demand was concentrated, why so many applicants weren't making it through, or when outreach should actually happen.

Raw sign-up counts couldn't answer any of that. They show volume, not outcome. I set out to answer three questions: where is participation actually concentrated, what's driving the gap between who applies and who gets in, and can future participation be predicted well enough to plan around it?

02 — The Data

8,558 learner records, 16 features, 70 countries.

Each row is one learner's registration for one opportunity — who they are, what they signed up for, when, and what happened to their application. The dataset spans five opportunity types and reaches learners across every populated continent.

05 — The Predictive Model

Descriptive analysis explains what happened. This model anticipates what happens next.

I framed this as a binary classification problem: will a learner participate (get placed, start, or complete) or not participate (get rejected, drop out, or withdraw)? A model that predicts this reliably lets program staff act before an applicant disengages, rather than finding out after the fact.

I trained and compared four models using an 80/20 stratified split, with hyperparameters tuned via 5-fold cross-validated grid search.

Selected model

Random Forest

Outperformed every alternative on every metric, and its ensemble structure resists overfitting far better than a single decision tree.

On the held-out test set (1,712 records)

The model correctly classified 1,473 of 1,712 test cases. It's noticeably better at avoiding false alarms than at catching every true participant — meaning it's more likely to under-flag a learner as "won't participate" than to over-promise. For a planning tool, that's a reasonable trade-off: it errs toward caution rather than false confidence.

In practice: a 92.35% precision score means when the model predicts participation, it's right 9 times out of 10 — reliable enough to act on for planning purposes.
06 — What Drives Participation?

The opportunity itself matters more than who's applying.

Feature importance from the Random Forest model — this reflects what the model leaned on most to make its predictions, not a claim about cause and effect.

What this means: Opportunity Category and Duration together account for over two-thirds of the model's predictive signal. The analysis suggests what is being offered and how long it runs shape participation more than any learner demographic — the model identified these as the strongest predictors, not proven causes.

08 — Results

What the analysis produced, and what it makes possible next.

These recommendations haven't been implemented yet — so I'm not claiming a measured business outcome. Here's the honest split between what was actually done and what it opens the door to.

Measured

  • 8,558 records analyzed across 70 countries and 5 opportunity categories
  • Key participation, seasonal, and geographic patterns identified and statistically confirmed
  • A validated Random Forest model — 86.04% accuracy, 0.932 ROC-AUC
  • Ranked feature importance identifying the strongest predictors of participation

What This Enables

  • Identifying learners least likely to progress, before it happens
  • Better-timed outreach and capacity planning around known seasonal peaks
  • Evidence-based targeting for geographic expansion
  • A basis for tracking future impact, if these recommendations are implemented
